Case study

Deloitte: machine learning for Fortune 100 clients

  • Role Machine Learning Consultant
  • Dates 2020–2022

ML and data work on Fortune 100 engagements: an ML contact-center product for Pfizer that cut average queue time from 75 to 15 minutes, and a clustering model for GM that saved $17.5M.

Confidentiality. Details are under NDA; this describes my role and approach, not proprietary specifics.

Context

From 2020 to 2022 I was a Machine Learning Consultant at Deloitte, working on engagements for Fortune 100 clients including Pfizer, General Motors and Walmart. At the same time I took graduate coursework in data science through Georgia Tech's OMSA program. I was promoted to senior in my first year, in the top 5% of my new-hire class.

The problem

Each engagement was a different problem. During COVID-19, a contact center was overwhelmed and wait times were long. A manufacturer was carrying configurations that cost more than they returned. A retailer needed its data moved onto a new platform without losing the ability to run analytics at scale.

My role

I worked on the machine learning and data side of consulting engagements, including hands-on ML engineering: managing the development of ML products, designing and deploying models, and designing data architecture. I also synthesized market research, competitive analysis and operational data into recommendations clients could act on.

What I built / did

  • Pfizer: managed the development of an ML-integrated contact-center product during COVID-19 that automated response generation for incoming requests.
  • General Motors: designed and deployed a clustering algorithm (unsupervised machine learning) to identify and prune low-value configurations.
  • Walmart: led a data migration to SAP S/4HANA and designed the data architecture for scalable analytics operations.

Results

  • Pfizer: average queue time fell from 75 minutes to 15.
  • General Motors: the configuration pruning saved $17.5M.
  • Promoted to senior in year one, top 5% of the new-hire class.

How I worked

Consulting taught me to start from the client's operating numbers and work back to the model, not the other way around. For each engagement I built the case from data the client already trusted, then designed the smallest system that would move the number that mattered: queue time, configuration cost, or analytics throughput. The artifacts were the usual consulting set: discovery findings, analysis decks, solution and data architecture designs, and recommendations written for executives.

What I'd do differently

I would push harder for measurement plans at the start of each engagement. When the baseline and the success metric are agreed in week one, the result is easier to defend and easier for the client to keep improving after the team leaves. I would also spend more time with the people who would run each system day to day, since they decide whether an ML product keeps working once the consultants are gone.
