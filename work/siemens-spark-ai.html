Case study

Siemens: an AI agent platform for the US workforce

  • Role AI Product Strategist, XPS Program
  • Dates 2026

Built and launched Spark AI, a 120+ agent platform for 1,000+ Siemens US employees across every major function, and presented a 3/6/12-month AI roadmap to US leadership.

Confidentiality. Details are under NDA; this describes my role and approach, not proprietary specifics.

Context

In summer 2026 I worked at Siemens as an AI Product Strategist in the XPS Program, focused on the Siemens US organization. The assignment was enterprise AI adoption: getting AI into the daily work of employees across every major function, not only engineering.

The problem

At the start of the summer, few people across the US organization used AI in their daily work. The work was to give employees in each function, at each level of seniority, AI tools built for their own jobs, get them to actually use those tools, and leave leadership with a plan for the next 12 months.

My role

My manager originated the idea for the platform. I built and launched it, drove adoption across the organization, and wrote and presented the roadmap for what should come next.

What I built / did

  • Built and launched Spark AI, a custom AI platform for Siemens US made up of 120+ custom agents and 50+ custom skills, built for specific job functions and seniority levels rather than as one general assistant.
  • Deployed it across 1,000+ employees spanning all major job functions.
  • Designed and presented a 3/6/12-month AI transformation roadmap to Siemens US leadership. It was sequenced by each function's readiness, mapped the dependencies between steps, and tied role-specific success metrics to measurable workflow efficiency outcomes.

How I worked

I worked from written documents: a product spec, phase-by-phase build checklists with acceptance criteria, a pre-launch checklist, and rollout readiness assessments with explicit go/no-go gates. Security, legal and privacy review came before a wide rollout, not after it. I wrote installation guides for end users so adoption did not depend on me being in the room. At the end of the summer I demoed the platform live to the team and walked through what worked and what did not.

What I'd do differently

In my end-of-summer review I named three things. First, more QA before each release. Second, a steadier shipping cadence with smaller releases, instead of large drops. Third, faster responses to stakeholders. I got feedback on responsiveness near the end of the summer, and it was fair. When people are deciding whether to rely on a new tool, how quickly its owner answers is part of the product.
