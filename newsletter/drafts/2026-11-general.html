Who we are

Our purpose is to develop disciple-leaders who use AI to enhance human flourishing. This newsletter is where we show the work: what shipped, what broke, and what you can take and use yourself.

Our top 10

Spark AI at Siemens: adoption is a change-management problem

This past summer I built and launched Spark AI for Siemens US: 120+ agents and 50+ skills, each built for a specific job function and seniority level, deployed to 1,000+ employees. My manager had the idea. I built it, launched it and drove adoption, and adoption was the real job. Active adoption rose from a 3% baseline to 65% in 10 weeks, and I left US leadership a 3/6/12-month roadmap sequenced by each function's readiness.

Read the case study

The BYU AI Foundry

The program itself. Students first learn to build their own product with AI coding agents, then work as builder consultants on live client engagements in the Foundry Lab. The client runs the software, and it ships under the students' names.

Visit aifoundry.byu.edu

AI Foundry Marketplace

Free, MIT-licensed plugins for Claude Code that the Foundry builds with: a full build process plus skills, agents and hooks. One command adds it, and every item carries an honest label for how well it has been tested, from verified to new. You can use it tonight.

Browse the marketplace

App Feedback Loop: what every Foundry client app ships with

Every app the Foundry ships includes the same feedback button. One click captures a screenshot and the context around it, Claude Code fixes the issue with that context loaded, and the fix commit is reported back to the person who filed it. It's an open-source build spec, not a library, so an agent implements it natively in any stack. For a client, the people using the software watch their own requests get fixed.

Read the spec

Nerve Center: 30+ agents taught me delegation

Nerve Center is the AI operating system I run my own work on: one orchestrator over 30+ agents in daily production, self-hosted on a Mac mini. Running it is mostly a management problem. Every job has an owner, anything irreversible waits at a gate until I say yes, and watchdogs keep checking after the work lands. The live brain page counts every agent and background job in real time, with names and data hidden.

See the live brain

recruit-copilot

A job search treated as a verification problem. It builds one experience bank from the resumes you already have, scores open roles against goals you set, tailors a resume per posting and checks that a machine can still read it. It never applies for you, by design.

Read the write-up

Clawdling

The open-source version of the idea behind Nerve Center. It runs locally on your own Claude plan, gives each project an agent and puts them on one map that shows who is working and who needs you. When an agent says it's done, you get a receipt. It's licensed AGPL-3.0.

Read the write-up

SpeedPrep: a product I build with my dad

My dad, John Davenport, is a career engineer, and SpeedPrep is our strongest co-build. It's a math speed game: every board asks one question, you spot the shortcut and snap the tiles, and the game tells you how fast your step was. It teaches 84 shortcuts that teachers use around the world, each one backed by real video lessons. He sets the product direction and makes the design calls, and I build and test it with agents.

Play SpeedPrep

Deckboard

Turns any screen into a split-flap display, the kind on old airport departure boards. It runs in the browser your TV already has, with photoreal flaps, sound engineered from the real machine and a documented API for pushing your own data. It's free, with no credit card. Another build with my dad.

Try Deckboard

AgentTree Merch: a store with two orders, both mine

shop.agenttree.army is a t-shirt store run by an agent loop: it designs, screens for trademark risk, lists products, takes payment and sends paid orders to the printer. As of mid-June it had two orders, both mine. The first one found three fulfillment bugs no mock had caught, then a fourth that charged the card and printed nothing. It works as a machine, but it is not a business yet.

What broke

Building in public means showing the misses too. Three I learned the most from:

  • Socrates, my X autopilot, is paused. It had its architecture months before it had safety rails that worked, and that order was the mistake.
  • The merch store's first order charged the card and printed nothing.
  • Version one of my voice-note pipeline was confidently wrong and polluted long-term memory. Most of the write-up is about precision.

Built with my dad

My dad, John Davenport, is a career engineer. SpeedPrep and Deckboard are above. Two more:

ce.rodeo

A free daily index that answers one question: which Docker image should I pull? It lines up every publisher's image for more than 150 open-source projects, scans each for known vulnerabilities and scores them with a published formula. It has a free public API, and my dad built it with Claude.

Visit ce.rodeo

ReadyStack

Verified Docker releases of open-source Community Edition software. A release counts only when a clean-room rebuild, with the network turned off, reproduces the identical image digest. The build kit is free with an account.

Visit ReadyStack
