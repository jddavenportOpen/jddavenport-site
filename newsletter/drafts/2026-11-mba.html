What a student AI studio at BYU Marriott shipped this fall, and what you can use tonight.

MBAs don't need to become engineers. We need to ship something a real client uses. At the AI Foundry that's a five-week engagement, and this is the first issue showing what came out of it.

Who we are

I'm JD Davenport, a BYU Marriott MBA candidate and the founder and Managing Director of the AI Foundry. The Foundry is an experiential learning program of BYU Strategy at the Marriott School: a studio where students build AI software for real organizations. This year it's 30 students, 15 MBAs and 15 undergrads, working in teams of one or two on five-week client engagements, with Scott Murff as faculty advisor.

I started it to give MBAs real reps building and shipping with AI agents. It has turned into a hybrid of a product studio and a management consulting firm, and I think that's the right shape for an MBA. Our purpose is to develop disciple-leaders who use AI to enhance human flourishing. We're a member of Anthropic's Claude Partner Network, and 175 people have joined our network so far.

Five builds, through an MBA lens

Spark AI at Siemens: adoption is a change-management problem

This past summer I built and launched Spark AI for Siemens US: 120+ agents and 50+ skills, each built for a specific job function and seniority level, deployed to 1,000+ employees. My manager had the idea. I built it, launched it and drove adoption, and adoption was the real job. Active adoption rose from a 3% baseline to 65% in 10 weeks, and I left US leadership a 3/6/12-month roadmap sequenced by each function's readiness.

Read the case study

AgentTree Merch: a store with two orders, both mine

shop.agenttree.army is a t-shirt store run by an agent loop: it designs, screens for trademark risk, lists products, takes payment and sends paid orders to the printer. As of mid-June it had two orders, both mine. The first one found three fulfillment bugs no mock had caught, then a fourth that charged the card and printed nothing. It works as a machine, but it is not a business yet.

Read the write-up

SpeedPrep

SpeedPrep is our strongest client build. It's a math speed game: every board asks one question, you spot the shortcut and snap the tiles, and the game tells you how fast your step was. It teaches 84 shortcuts that teachers use around the world, each one backed by real video lessons.

Play SpeedPrep

Nerve Center: 30+ agents taught me delegation

Nerve Center is the AI operating system I run my own work on: one orchestrator over 30+ agents in daily production, self-hosted on a Mac mini. Running it is mostly a management problem. Every job has an owner, anything irreversible waits at a gate until I say yes, and watchdogs keep checking after the work lands. The live brain page counts every agent and background job in real time, with names and data hidden.

See the live brain

App Feedback Loop: what every Foundry client app ships with

Every app the Foundry ships includes the same feedback button. One click captures a screenshot and the context around it, Claude Code fixes the issue with that context loaded, and the fix commit is reported back to the person who filed it. It's an open-source build spec, not a library, so an agent implements it natively in any stack. For a client, the people using the software watch their own requests get fixed.

How an engagement works

An organization tells us what it wants built. We come back with a written scope, a cost estimate and a delivery timeline. One or two builders take it for five weeks, extendable, with faculty oversight on every engagement. The client runs the software, and it ships under the students' names.

Class time is delivery. The Foundry Lab (MBA 693R and STRAT 490R) meets Fridays from 8:00 to 11:50 AM to work live client problems. Students earn three credit hours a semester, get a Claude plan for Foundry work and leave with a client contact who can speak to their work. Read our approach.

Take this tonight

recruit-copilot is for recruiting season. It merges the resumes you already have into one experience bank, scores open roles against goals you set, tailors a resume per posting and checks that a machine can still read it. It never applies for you. The marketplace labels it beta. Paste this into a terminal with Claude Code installed:

claude plugin marketplace add jddavenportOpen/recruit-copilot && claude plugin install recruit@recruit-copilot

Building something? Add the AI Foundry marketplace once, then type /plugin inside Claude Code to browse:

claude plugin marketplace add ai-foundry-byu/foundry-marketplace

The full list, 37 free items and 6 workflows with honest test labels, is at jddavenport.com/docs/marketplace.
